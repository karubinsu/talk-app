# Tandem — Friday Event Onboarding & Founder Guide

**Purpose:** A practical guide for the upcoming event.

The goal is **validation, not a launch**.

You are testing whether people understand the problem, whether the conversation experience feels useful, whether they would use Tandem in real situations, and what feels awkward or unnecessary.

You do not need to prove that the final product already exists.

## 1. Onboarding screen

The onboarding screen has one job:

> **Make someone understand why Tandem could be useful within a few seconds.**

Recommended structure:

**Tandem**

**Connect Beyond Language**

> **Talk to people who speak a different language, using the language you're comfortable with.**

**Try an in-person conversation**

> **Use one phone to chat with someone who speaks another language.**

**Coming next**

> **Connect on your own phones and keep the conversation going after you meet.**

Optional:

> **Useful for:** International meetups · Travel · Restaurants · Meeting new people

Footer:

> **This is an early version. Try it and tell me what you think.**

### Do not put on the onboarding screen

Do not list QR pairing, Google login, persistent conversations, AI summaries, tone controls, voice input, translation providers, backend technology, or the full roadmap.

Communicate the experience, not the engineering plan.

## 2. Your initial explanation

Say:

> **"I'm building an app called Tandem. The idea is to help people communicate when they don't share a comfortable language. This is an early version, so right now I'm testing it using one phone. I'd love for you to try it and tell me what feels useful or awkward."**

Then stop talking and let them use it.

Do not give a long pitch before they touch it.

## 3. If they ask why you are passing one phone

Say:

> **"That's just how this early version works. I'm testing the core conversation experience first. The direction I'm working toward is having both people use their own phones."**

If they ask whether that is final:

> **"No. I actually think using your own phones will be much more natural. The one-phone version is just the simplest prototype for testing it here."**

## 4. If they ask about the future two-phone experience

Say:

> **"Yes. That's the direction. One person would start a conversation and invite the other person, likely through a QR code. Each person would choose the language they're comfortable with, then we'd both have the conversation on our own phones."**

For staying connected:

> **"That's another part of the vision. If you meet someone through Tandem and want to keep talking, the conversation could continue through the app instead of moving to a normal messenger where the language barrier comes back."**

Do not promise dates.

## 5. If they ask how the translation works

Start with:

> **"Right now, I'm using a translation service behind the app to handle the language conversion. I'm still experimenting with the implementation, so I'm keeping the technical details private for now."**

If they ask whether AI is involved:

> **"Yes, there's AI involved in the translation experience, but I'm more interested right now in whether the conversation itself feels natural and useful."**

If you do not want to name the provider:

> **"I'm still testing different approaches, so I don't want to get too deep into the implementation yet."**

For a developer who genuinely wants the architecture:

> **"The app handles the conversation and language settings, sends the message through the translation layer, then presents the translated result to the other person."**

You do not owe anyone the technical recipe.

## 6. If they ask what the big idea is

Say:

> **"I don't want translation to be the product. I want communication to be the product. The translation is what makes it possible when two people don't share a comfortable language."**

## 7. If they ask why not Google Translate

Say:

> **"That's a fair question. Translation already exists. What I'm exploring is making the translation part of an actual conversation between two people, especially when they meet and want to keep talking afterward."**

Do not claim that Tandem replaces existing translation products.

## 8. If they ask who it is for

Say:

> **"Anyone who wants to communicate with someone they can't comfortably communicate with in the same language."**

Then give one or two examples:

> **"It could be someone at an international meetup, a traveler talking to a local, or a restaurant guest talking to staff."**

Do not reduce the product to English speakers or non-English speakers.

## 9. If they ask about future features

Lead with the two most important:

> **"The biggest direction is letting both people use their own phones instead of sharing one. Then, if they want to stay connected, the conversation can continue through the app."**

If they are interested, mention:

> **"I'm also thinking about things like voice input for in-person conversations, conversation history, and optional summaries that can help people remember what they talked about."**

Describe these as development directions, not promises.

## 10. If they ask about summaries

Say:

> **"The idea isn't to automatically summarize everything. If you want to reconnect with someone later, you could ask Tandem to give you a quick summary of who they are and what you talked about before. That could make it easier to pick up the conversation again."**

## 11. If they ask about accounts

Say:

> **"Ideally, the person starting the conversation would already have their setup. The other person could scan a QR code, enter their name and language, and get into the conversation with as little friction as possible. Account creation would depend on what they want to do with the app afterward."**

Do not imply that a particular authentication system is already built.

## 12. Questions to ask users

Do not only ask "Do you like it?"

Use specific questions:

1. **"What felt awkward?"**
2. **"What felt surprisingly useful?"**
3. **"Would you actually use this outside this event?"**
4. **"Where would you personally use it?"**
5. **"Would you rather use your own phone or share one?"**
6. **"If you met someone through this, would you want to keep talking through the app?"**
7. **"What would make you stop using it?"**

Ask two or three rather than interrogating everyone.

## 13. What to observe

Watch for:

- Whether they understand what to do without explanation
- Whether they know whose turn it is
- Hesitation before typing
- Confusion about language selection
- Discomfort passing the phone
- Whether they read the translation carefully
- Positive reactions to successful communication
- Whether they independently ask for two-phone use
- Whether they ask if they can stay connected
- Whether they spontaneously suggest other use cases

Especially notice if someone independently says:

> **"It would be better if we could both use our phones."**

That is strong validation for the direction.

## 14. How much to say

Use this rule:

> **Explain the current experience. Briefly explain the future. Let the user discover the rest.**

Aim for roughly **20% talking / 80% letting them use and react**.

You are there to learn.

## 15. If someone is very interested

Say:

> **"The bigger vision is for Tandem to become a way for people to communicate across languages, not just a translation tool. You could meet someone, connect through the app, talk using your own languages, and then keep the conversation going afterward without needing a shared language."**

Then ask:

> **"What would you expect an app like that to do?"**

Turn their enthusiasm into research.

## 16. What not to claim

Avoid:

- "This will replace WhatsApp."
- "This will become the universal translator."
- "Everyone will be able to communicate with anyone."
- "The translation is always accurate."
- "The app will definitely have feature X."

Prefer:

- "That's the direction I'm exploring."
- "That's something I want to build."
- "I'm testing whether people actually want this."
- "That's part of the future vision."

## 17. Your 30-second version

Memorize:

> **"I'm building Tandem, an app that helps people communicate when they don't share a comfortable language. Right now I'm testing an early version where two people can use one phone to have a conversation in their own languages. The bigger direction is letting both people use their own phones, then continue talking through the app afterward. I'm testing it here because I want to see whether people actually find that useful and what feels awkward."**

## 18. Your one-sentence version

If someone asks:

> **"What is Tandem?"**

Say:

> **"It's an app that helps two people communicate when they don't share a comfortable language."**

Then let them ask the next question.

## 19. The mindset

You are **not launching a finished company on Friday**.

You are putting an early product in front of roughly 20 real people and watching what happens.

Your job is not to convince everyone Tandem is brilliant.

Your job is to discover:

> **"Is there something here that people actually want?"**

If someone dislikes the one-phone experience, that is useful.

If someone immediately asks for two-phone pairing, that is useful.

If someone suggests restaurants, travel, or other use cases, that is useful.

If someone says they would never use it, that is useful.

If someone asks:

> **"Can I keep talking to this person through the app afterward?"**

Pay very close attention. That is strong evidence that the broader product vision is resonating.

## Final rule

**Show the working prototype.**

**Explain the problem clearly.**

**Show the vision briefly.**

**Do not reveal unnecessary technical details.**

**Ask concrete questions.**

**Write down the answers immediately after each interaction.**

**Do not defend criticism.**

**Do not promise features or dates.**

**Do not pretend the prototype is the final product.**

The goal is not to protect the idea by hiding it.

The goal is to learn faster than someone else could copy it.
